How it works

Three steps, and the tuna does the explaining.

  1. Pick your instrument and tuningTap play to hear the whole tuning before you start.
  2. Pluck a stringBig Tuna hears which string it is and shows whether it's too low or too high, and by how much.
  3. Turn the pegTighten or loosen until the trail sits in the green and the string lights up.

Tunes the string you meant

Most tuner mistakes happen before you've touched a peg: it hears the wrong string, or the wrong octave. Big Tuna is built to catch those.

Hears which string you play

Auto mode finds the string you plucked, even while the others ring. Tap a string to hear it and lock onto it.

Speaks up when you're on the wrong string

Locked to the A but plucking the D? Big Tuna tells you what it hears instead of pushing you to over-tighten.

Never snaps a string

Octave misreads are caught, so it won't talk you into cranking a string up a whole octave.

Knows which way you turned

Turned the peg the wrong way? It says so, before you've gone a semitone in the wrong direction.

Common questions

Is The Big Tuna really free?

Yes. Tuning every instrument and every tuning is free, with no ads and no account. Big Tuna Pro is an optional one-time purchase that adds extras like strum mode, custom tunings and setlists.

When can I download it?

Big Tuna is coming to the App Store and Google Play. On Android you can use it today by joining the free beta.

Does it record me?

No. It listens only while the tuner is open, analyzes the sound on your phone in real time, and never records, stores or sends it anywhere.

Can it tune to 432 Hz or 442 Hz?

Yes. Settings → Calibration (A4) sets the reference pitch anywhere from 415 to 466 Hz.

Does it work for bass, ukulele and violin?

Yes: 4, 5 and 6 string bass, soprano, concert, tenor, baritone and bass ukulele, banjo, tenor banjo, mandolin, violin, viola and cello, plus a chromatic mode for anything else.

My tuner jumps around on the low strings. Will this help?

That's exactly what Big Tuna is built to handle: it catches octave misreads and picks out the string you plucked while others ring. Plucking one string firmly, close to the phone, helps any tuner.
